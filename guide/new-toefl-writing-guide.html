New TOEFL Writing (2026): All Three Tasks Explained

The Writing section of the redesigned TOEFL iBT (January 2026 format) no longer revolves around a single long essay. Instead, it combines three shorter, more practical tasks — each testing a different layer of writing ability.

TaskWhat you doWhat it tests
Build a SentenceArrange jumbled words and phrases into a correct sentence that fits a given context or answers a question.Grammar and word order accuracy, at speed.
Write an EmailWrite a short, appropriate email in an everyday academic situation (to a professor, an office, a classmate).Practical writing: tone, organisation, covering all the points in the prompt.
Write for an Academic DiscussionContribute a post to an online class discussion, responding to a professor's question and classmates' posts.Argument writing: stating a position, supporting it, and engaging with others' views.

How to prepare for each task

Build a Sentence: drill the patterns

This is the most mechanical of the three — and the most improvable. The same structures recur: indirect questions, embedded clauses, passives, comparatives, and modifier placement. Short daily drills build the reflexes. See our task explainer and word-order strategies.

Write an Email: learn the shape

Successful emails follow a predictable shape: a polite opening line, one short paragraph per point in the prompt, and a courteous close. Practice by writing to a checklist — every bullet in the prompt must appear in your email — and by keeping sentences simple and direct.

Academic Discussion: have a template of moves

You typically need around 100+ words that state your position, give a reason and an example, and respond to a classmate's idea. Prepare a small set of flexible moves — agreeing with a point and extending it, or respectfully disagreeing with a reason — so you can spend your time on content, not structure.
