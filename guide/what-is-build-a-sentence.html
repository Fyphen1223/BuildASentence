What Is the TOEFL "Build a Sentence" Task?

Build a Sentence is one of the three tasks in the Writing section of the redesigned TOEFL iBT (January 2026 format). You are given a short context — typically a question or a statement from a conversation — and a set of jumbled words and phrases. Your job: arrange them into a grammatically correct sentence that fits the context.

How the task works

ElementDetails
SectionWriting (new TOEFL iBT format)
Your taskDrag/tap the given words and phrases into the correct order
ContextEach item follows a short prompt, often a question you are answering
What it testsGrammar and word order: questions, embedded clauses, reported speech, passives, modifiers
PaceFast — many short items, seconds each, rather than one long essay

Why this task is trickier than it looks

Most items are not simple subject–verb–object sentences. The difficult patterns are the ones where English word order is rigid but unfamiliar: indirect questions ("Do you know where the library is?" — not "where is the library"), embedded clauses, and long modifiers that must attach to the right noun. Under time pressure, even advanced learners produce orders that are correct in their first language but wrong in English.

Key point: Build a Sentence is a pure accuracy-and-speed task. There is no partial credit for a "mostly right" order — the sentence is either grammatical or it is not. That makes it one of the most trainable tasks on the test.

Strategies that work

  • Find the core first: locate the subject and the main verb, place them, then attach everything else.
  • Check the prompt type: if you are answering a question, decide whether the answer needs a statement order or an embedded-question order before you start moving words.
  • Attach modifiers last: prepositional phrases and relative clauses go after the core is stable — and next to the word they modify.
  • Read the finished sentence once: a two-second read-through catches most order slips.
